# December newsletter planner pick

**Pick: Year Start Goal Planner.** Its sales are rising into December, while the best seller's sales slow in December.

## Three reasons (from Shop/products.md)

1. **Sales are climbing.** Shop/products.md line 8 (Year Start Goal Planner row): sold 35 in October and 96 in November, and the Notes say "Sales rise in November and December."
2. **The leading alternative slows in December.** Line 7 (Weekly Focus Planner row): Notes say "Sales slow every December." Its sales also fell from 140 to 122 between October and November. It is still ahead of Year Start in November (122 vs 96).
3. **The third planner has a known complaint.** Line 9 (Daily Habit Tracker row): average rating 3.9, the lowest of the three (4.6 and 4.4 on lines 7 and 8), and the Notes say several reviews find the boxes too small to write in. Its sales were flat (60 in October, 58 in November).

## What the file does not say

From the "Things the list does not say" section (lines 11-15): there is no data on why customers bought, no figures from last year, and returns are not recorded. The "sales rise every December" pattern therefore rests on the Notes text alone, with no numbers behind it.

## My read

- The Year Start planner is dated for next year (line 8 Notes), which fits a December send. This is my judgement, not something the file states.
- The case would be stronger with last year's December sales and the reasons customers buy. Without them, the pick is a reading of two months of sales plus the Notes.
- If HQ wants to promote the planner with the most sales and best reviews, that is the Weekly Focus Planner (lines 7). That is a different goal from the one I assumed, which was to feature the planner with the best December outlook. HQ and the owner decide.
